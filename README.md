## The Core Problem: Tribal Knowledge and Brain Drain

Before we begin, let us ask you a simple question:  
*For your particular business—whether tech or non-tech—have you ever heard an employee say: **"Ask that person, he/she has all the knowledge about that"**?*

Now, think to yourself:  
**What happens if the person who knows everything about your system resigns tomorrow? What will be affected?**

It is very common in engineering companies today. A senior engineer who has been handling the most critical systems of the company for the last 3 years submits an immediate resignation.

- The person leaves, but their code is still in GitHub.
- **What about the knowledge and logic in their brain?** Why was a decision taken? What components are fragile (easily broken)? Where are the single points of failure?  
- **All of that critical context walks out the door with them.**

This is called **Tribal Knowledge** — the single greatest unquantified financial liability in modern tech companies.

When a new engineer joins the company, it easily takes **2 to 4 weeks** just to understand who owns what. And when something breaks in production, only then does the company realize how critical that departed person was.

You can ask Cortex in plain English:

These numbers are **not randomly generated by an AI**. They are purely calculated using deterministic mathematical formulas from your real company data. AI is only a presentation layer for understanding user queries. That is why **Cortex never guesses: if data exists, it calculates; if not, it never hallucinates.**

## The 4 Core Value Pillars (Why You Need This Today)

### 1. Knowledge Departure Risk and Succession Insurance
* **The Problem:** You have no idea what will break if a key engineer departs, nor who has the skills to take over.
* **The Cortex Solution:** Cortex computes a deterministic **6-Factor Departure Risk Score (0–100%)** combining code ownership, active dependencies, documentation gaps, and sole-contributor items.
* **The Successor Engine:** Automatically evaluates all team members across **4 dimensions** (*Technology Jaccard Overlap, Repository Overlap, 30-Day Activity Decay, and Current Workload Capacity*) to recommend the exact best successor with a concrete percentage match.

### 2. Real-Time Bus Factor and SPOF Elimination
* **The Problem:** Critical microservices often have only 1 person who understands them, creating massive Single Points of Failure (SPOF).
* **The Cortex Solution:** Cortex constantly evaluates the **Bus Factor** for every single repository across your organization. It flags fragile repositories where a single engineer accounts for more than 50% of commits before it turns into an emergency.

### 3. 70% Reduction in Developer Onboarding Time
* **The Problem:** New hires spend 30% of their first two months asking teammates *"Who owns this?"*, *"Where is this documented?"*, or reading outdated Confluence pages.
* **The Cortex Solution:** New engineers ask Cortex: *"Who is the expert on our billing tokenization?"* or *"What services depend on auth-service?"* and receive immediate dependency maps and verified code links.

### 4. Architectural Archaeology ("The Decision Memory")
* **The Problem:** Teams make architectural decisions in Slack threads or Jira comments that get forgotten 6 months later, leading to repeated mistakes.
* **The Cortex Solution:** Ask Cortex: *"Why did we migrate our caching layer from Redis to Valkey last quarter?"* Cortex cross-references the GitHub PR, the Slack debate, and the Jira ticket to give you the exact rationale and merged commit history.

Cortex operates under a strict **"Calculator vs. Formatter"** architecture:

```text

  Neo4j Graph Math + PostgreSQL + Qdrant
                   │
                   ▼
         VERIFIED EVIDENCE PAYLOAD
  (Exact Commit Hashes, Bus Factor 1, Risk 20%)
                   │
                   ▼
          LLM SYNTHESIS LAYER
    (Strict Zero-Fabrication Prompt)
                   │
                   ▼
        100% GROUNDED CLIENT ANSWER
```

1. **Zero LLM Guesswork in Calculations:**  
   Risk percentages, bus factors, and successor rankings are **not** created by an LLM. They are computed using pure mathematical formulas in TypeScript and Neo4j Cypher algorithms.
2. **Traceable Source Evidence:**  
   Every claim, metric, and percentage is anchored to real commit hashes, Jira ticket keys, and Slack message timestamps.
3. **Negative Constraint Honesty:**  
   If an engineer has zero contributions in a domain, Cortex explicitly states: *"No candidate with overlapping technologies was found."* It will never fabricate a fake successor or non-existent repository.

## Deep Dive: Cortex vs. Key Market Categories

### 1. Cortex vs. Enterprise Search ($7B+ Valuation Category — e.g., Glean, Coveo)
* **What they do:** Index Google Drive, Confluence, Slack, and Jira to help marketing, HR, and sales find documents.
* **Where they fail for Engineering:** They treat code as plain text. They cannot trace that *Commit A* modified *Microservice B* which uses *Technology C* that depends on *Engineer D*. They cannot answer *"What breaks if Rohan leaves?"*
* **Cortex's Edge:** Cortex is purpose-built for engineering architecture with a live Neo4j graph database, calculating technical dependencies and ownership metrics that generic document search engines cannot comprehend.

### 2. Cortex vs. Code Intelligence ($2B+ Category — e.g., Sourcegraph, GitHub Enterprise)
* **What they do:** Excellent at finding specific syntax across 1,000 repositories (e.g., *"Where is function `authToken()` defined?"*).
* **Where they fail for Engineering:** They operate purely inside the git repository. They have zero visibility into why a decision was made on Slack, what Jira ticket approved the migration, or which engineer has the cognitive bandwidth to inherit a legacy repo.
* **Cortex's Edge:** Cortex fuses version control with conversational context (Slack/Jira), turning cold source code into an active knowledge memory.

### 3. Cortex vs. Engineering Management Platforms (e.g., Jellyfish, LinearB, Swarmia)
* **What they do:** Provide retroactive charts showing sprint velocity, average review time, and engineering cost allocation.
* **Where they fail for Engineering:** They are passive dashboards. They tell you that a project is late, but they cannot proactively simulate key-person departure, suggest exact skill-matched successors, or answer ad-hoc architectural questions.
* **Cortex's Edge:** Cortex is an interactive, autonomous agent. Instead of looking at 50 static charts, a CTO can simply ask questions in plain English and receive instant, mathematically verified answers with zero hallucination.
